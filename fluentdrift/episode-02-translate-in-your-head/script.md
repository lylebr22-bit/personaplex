# Episode 2 - Why You Still Translate in Your Head (3-Step Fix)

**Runtime target:** 7:30
**Narration:** 145 wpm, ElevenLabs Brian
**Word count:** ~1,090 spoken words

---

## Scene 1 [0:00 - 0:30] - Cold open

[Music OFF. Tight ambient. Confiding tone.]

> Right now, while you're listening to me, your brain is doing two
> things at once.

[Beat. 0.6s pause.]

> It's hearing me. And it's translating me. Even though you don't
> want it to.

[On-screen text: `You translate every sentence. That's the leak.`]

---

## Scene 2 [0:30 - 1:00] - The cost

> Translation steals about three hundred milliseconds from every
> sentence you hear. Three hundred milliseconds doesn't sound like
> much. But over a five-minute conversation, that is two full
> minutes of lag in your head.

> Two minutes where natives think you're slow. Where you think
> you're stupid. And where the conversation moves on without you.

---

## Scene 3 [1:00 - 1:30] - The diagnosis

> Here's how you know translation is your bottleneck. Three signs.

> One. You understand what someone said, but you forget it before
> you can reply.

> Two. You can write English fine, but speaking feels three times
> harder.

> Three. You watch a movie and feel exhausted afterward.

> If even one of those is you, you have a translation habit. And
> the next seven minutes will fix it.

---

## Scene 4 [1:30 - 2:00] - Why it happens

[Teaching tone. Even pacing.]

> Translation is a habit your brain learned at the very beginning
> of English class. The teacher said apple. They showed a picture
> of an apple. They said the word in your language. Your brain
> wired apple to your mother tongue, not to the actual fruit.

> Every word you learned before fifteen years old has this same
> wiring. That is your translation network. And it fires
> automatically.

---

## Scene 5 [2:00 - 2:30] - The fix overview

> The fix is not "stop translating." That's like telling someone
> to stop sweating. You can't will it away. You have to
> rewire the connection.

> Three steps. Each takes one minute. You do all three, every
> day, for fourteen days. By day fifteen, your default for English
> stops being your mother tongue. It becomes images.

> Step one. Object naming.

---

## Scene 6 [2:30 - 3:00] - PATTERN INTERRUPT 1

[Hard cut. Music OFF. Black screen.]

> Pause this video. Look at the closest object to you. A cup. A
> phone. A pen. Whatever.

> Now in your head, do not say its name. Just see it. Hold the
> picture in your mind for five seconds.

[3-second visible silence. Timer 00:05 -> 00:00.]

> That feeling - the picture without a word - is what we are
> training. Welcome back.

---

## Scene 7 [3:00 - 3:30] - Step 1 detailed

[Curious teacher tone. Warmer.]

> Step one is called Object Naming. One minute a day.

> You walk through a room. For every object you see, you name
> it out loud in English. Just the noun.

> Cup. Lamp. Phone. Window. Plant. Door.

> No sentences. No translation. Just the English word, fired
> directly at the object you see. After fourteen days, your
> brain stops routing through your mother tongue for nouns. They
> become images that have an English label.

---

## Scene 8 [3:30 - 4:00] - Step 2 setup

> Step two. Verb mapping.

> Same idea. But for actions, not objects. While you walk to
> work, while you cook, while you brush your teeth. Narrate
> yourself out loud, in English, in present tense.

> I am walking. I am opening the door. I am stepping outside. I
> am breathing.

> Boring? Yes. Effective? Insanely.

---

## Scene 9 [4:00 - 4:30] - PATTERN INTERRUPT 2

[Record scratch. Red flash.]

> Stanford studied this exact technique in two thousand twenty
> two. Forty intermediate learners did one minute of object
> naming and one minute of verb mapping daily for two weeks.

> Their reaction time when speaking dropped by forty-one percent.
> Forty-one. After fourteen days. From two minutes.

> That is the cheapest fluency upgrade ever discovered.

---

## Scene 10 [4:30 - 5:00] - Step 3 setup

[Gentle correction tone. Slowest section.]

> Step three is the hardest. And the most important.

> It is called Image Replacement.

> When someone says a sentence to you in English, do not translate
> it. Picture it. Build a mental image of what they said.

> Someone says I went to the store yesterday. Don't translate it.
> See them, walking, into a store, in your mind.

---

## Scene 11 [5:00 - 5:30] - Why it works

> Translation is a verbal process. Imagery is a visual process.
> Your brain processes images about sixty times faster than words.

> When you turn English into pictures instead of words, you skip
> the translation step entirely. You do not hear English and
> translate it. You hear English and see it. The way natives do.

> This is the actual definition of fluency.

---

## Scene 12 [5:30 - 6:00] - The combined drill

> So your full daily routine is three minutes.

> One minute object naming - rewire nouns.
> One minute verb mapping - rewire actions.
> One minute image replacement - practice the bridge.

> Three minutes. Fourteen days. The translation habit dies.

---
