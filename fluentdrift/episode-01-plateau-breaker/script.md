# Episode 1 - Why You're Stuck at B1 (And the 3-Min Fix)

**Runtime target:** 8:00
**Narration:** 145 wpm, ElevenLabs Brian
**Word count:** ~1,180 spoken words

Each block is a scene in the storyboard. Lines prefixed with `>` are
spoken by the narrator. Bracketed notes `[...]` are production
directions for the editor and voice engineer, not spoken.

---

## Scene 1 [0:00 - 0:30] - Cold open

[Music OFF. Clock ticking only. Low register, confiding tone.]

> You're not a beginner anymore. But every time you open your mouth
> in English, you feel like one.

[Beat. 0.8s pause.]

> That's not your fault. And in the next eight minutes, I'll prove it.

[On-screen text fades in: `You're stuck at B1 or B2. This is why.`]

## Scene 2 [0:30 - 1:00] - Stakes

[Ambient synth pad enters softly. Baseline grounded-authority tone.]

> There are one point five billion English learners in the world.
> Eighty percent of them will stay intermediate forever. They'll
> study for ten, fifteen, twenty more years. And never cross the
> line to fluent.

> Not because they're lazy. Not because they're bad at languages.
> But because they're doing three things that worked at A1. And are
> now quietly destroying their progress.

---

## Scene 3 [1:00 - 1:30] - Mistake 1 intro

[Teaching tone. Clear enunciation.]

> I'm going to show you those three things. And I'm going to give
> you one drill for each. Drills you can do today. In your kitchen.
> With no teacher. By the end of this episode, your English brain
> will already be different.

> Let's start. Mistake number one. You are still studying English.

---

## Scene 4 [1:30 - 2:00] - The study trap

> When you were a beginner, studying worked. Flashcards worked.
> Grammar books worked. You went from zero to basic conversations.
> Amazing. So your brain learned a rule. More study equals more
> English.

> Here's what the research actually says. After the B1 level,
> adding more study hours gives you almost zero speaking
> improvement. Zero. You can do another five hundred lessons and
> sound exactly the same in six months.

---

## Scene 5 [2:00 - 2:30] - Drill 1 setup

> Why? Because fluency is not knowledge. It is speed. And speed
> only comes from one thing. Output under pressure.

> Your first drill is called the Two-Minute Monologue.

[On-screen sticky bar: `Drill 1: 2-Minute Monologue - daily`]

> Every morning, before coffee, before your phone, you set a timer
> for two minutes. And you talk out loud, in English, to nobody.
> Any topic. Your dreams, your grocery list, why you hate Mondays.
> No stopping. No correcting.

---

## Scene 7 [3:00 - 3:30] - Mistake 2 intro

[Curious teacher tone. Warmer.]

> Second mistake. Intermediate learners collect words. Fluent
> speakers collect chunks.

> A chunk is a block of words that natives say together
> automatically. Without thinking. *To be honest. I was just about
> to. It kind of depends. What do you mean by that.*

> Natives do not build those sentences word by word. They grab
> them whole. Like LEGO blocks that are already snapped together.

---

## Scene 8 [3:30 - 4:00] - Drill 2

> The average native speaker uses the same five hundred chunks in
> eighty percent of their daily conversations. Five hundred
> chunks. That's it. That is the real vocabulary of fluency. Not
> ten thousand rare words. Five hundred everyday blocks.

> Your second drill is called Chunk Mining.

[On-screen sticky bar: `Drill 2: Mine 10 chunks / 1 video / day`]

> Pick one YouTube video in English. Ten minutes long. Watch it
> with subtitles. Write down every three to five word phrase that
> sounds natural. Say each one out loud five times. Do this for
> thirty days. You will have three hundred native chunks locked
> in your mouth.

---

## Scene 9 [4:00 - 4:30] - PATTERN INTERRUPT 2

[Record scratch. Red flash. Firm challenge delivery.]

> Everything you learned about grammar is slowing you down.

[Beat.]

[Back to grounded authority tone.]

> I know. Your teacher won't like this. But a twenty nineteen study
> from Cambridge tracked four hundred intermediate learners for
> two years. Half did more grammar drills. Half did more listening
> and speaking. The speakers became fluent four times faster. Four
> times. Grammar is a map. But you don't learn to walk by reading
> maps. You walk. Back to the drill.

---

## Scene 10 [4:30 - 5:00] - Mistake 3 intro

[Gentle correction tone. Slowest section. 135 wpm.]

> Third and biggest mistake. You listen to English passively.

> You put on a podcast while you cook. You watch Netflix with
> subtitles. You tell yourself this counts.

> It doesn't. Not for speaking.

---

## Scene 11 [5:00 - 5:30] - Why passive fails

> Passive listening trains your comprehension. It does almost
> nothing for your production. Your mouth does not know the word
> unless your mouth has said the word.

> This is why some intermediate learners can understand TED Talks
> but cannot order coffee smoothly. The two skills live in
> different parts of the brain.

---

## Scene 12 [5:30 - 6:00] - Drill 3 shadowing

> Your third drill is called Shadowing. The one-second version.

[On-screen sticky bar: `Drill 3: 10 min shadowing - daily`]

> You pick a short audio clip. Fifteen to thirty seconds. Of a
> native speaker. You play it. You pause. And within one second,
> you repeat it out loud. Copying the melody, the speed, the
> stress, even the breathing. Not translating. Not thinking. Just
> echoing.

> One second is the key. It forces your brain to bypass
> translation. And hardwire the sound into your mouth.

---
